Co-authored Research Projects

Network Transmission of Fiscal Demand Shocks in Commodity-Dependent Economies
With Carlos Uribe-Terán, Wladimir Zanoni, Diego F. Grijalva, and Osmel Manzano. IDB Working Paper No. IDB-WP-01793, Inter-American Development Bank, 2026.
Paper

We study how procurement-driven fiscal contractions propagate through firm-to-firm production networks. The 2014 oil price collapse triggered a sharp fall in public procurement in Ecuador, generating an externally driven fiscal demand shock. Using administrative data covering the formal-firm universe from 2012 to 2019, we construct exposure measures that trace the shock from government contractors to their trading partners. Aggregating the causal estimates implies a network-adjusted fiscal multiplier of 1.95: a one-dollar procurement reduction lowers aggregate value added by US$1.95, compared with US$1.18 when network propagation is ignored. Production linkages therefore account for about 39 percent of the total response.

From Preferences to Reciprocity: Firm Adjustment after the EU–Ecuador Free Trade Agreement
With Carlos Uribe-Terán and Diego Grijalva. Book chapter in Las relaciones Ecuador–Unión Europea en el siglo XXI.

The 2017 trade agreement between Ecuador and the European Union marked a turning point in the bilateral relationship. It replaced a system based on unilateral trade preferences with one based on reciprocity, preserving Ecuador's access to the European market while gradually opening the Ecuadorian market to European goods. This chapter examines how that new phase of integration affected firms operating in Ecuador. Because the agreement largely maintained existing export access while gradually reducing Ecuador's tariffs on European products, its most visible effects were expected on the import side. Using customs and firm-level financial records, the chapter shows that the agreement did not transform firms overnight. They emerged gradually, as lower tariffs made European goods more accessible. Firms more exposed to tariff reductions increased their imports from Europe and, over time, also expanded their use of materials, sales, and employment, i.e., they experienced a positive scale-effect. In contrast, we find no evidence that exposure to lower tariffs translated into higher productivity. The Ecuadorian case offers a broader lesson about the transition from preferences to reciprocity in the European Union's relations with developing economies: the domestic effects of these agreements may be real, but they often appear slowly.

Undergraduate Thesis

Poverty Traps and Intergenerational Mobility: Evidence from 33 Years of Labor Surveys for Ecuador
Universidad San Francisco de Quito, 2024. Awarded Best Undergraduate Research Paper by the School of Economics, USFQ.
Thesis

This paper explores Ecuador's labor income dynamics from 1990 to 2022 around the presence of poverty traps and the type of intergenerational mobility within the country's labor market. The study uses a pseudo-panel approach to estimate nonlinear dynamics in labor income, looking at the relationship between past and present income levels across approximately 16 generations, or cohorts. It employs semi-parametric Gaussian Kernel estimations using labor income lagged by 1, 3, 5, and 10 years. The findings do not reveal evidence supporting poverty traps in labor income. Furthermore, the analysis identifies significant levels of conditional temporal labor income immobility across diverse demographic cohorts, regardless of educational attainment. These results reinforce the idea that specific demographic groups, while experiencing some level of mobility, still face significant obstacles in moving up the income ladder in Ecuador. This study contributes insights for policymakers and researchers seeking to address income inequality in Ecuador and similar contexts.
